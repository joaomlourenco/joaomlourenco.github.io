other: "Concurrent Systems covers the principles and practice of shared-memory multiprocessor programming, based on The Art of Multiprocessor Programming. The theory runs from mutual exclusion and the correctness and progress of concurrent objects (linearizability, lock- and wait-freedom), through consensus and the relative power of synchronization primitives, to practical techniques: spin locks and contention, fine-grained and lock-free data structures, data races and deadlocks, and transactional memory. In the practical classes, students solve exercises that put these concepts into practice and develop a project in groups of two: implementing and evaluating a concurrent algorithm or system. The course aims to make students able to reason rigorously about concurrent algorithms and to build correct, efficient concurrent software."
